A short biography of someone who refuses to pick a lane.

§ 02.1
Discipline

By day I am a software engineer — shipping into a component library used inside Apple, authoring framework-agnostic Web Components, building Node and Laravel services behind dashboards nobody sees, and packaging TypeScript primitives that other teams import and forget. The work is mostly unglamorous and that's the point. Good software is a long series of small, careful decisions made in sequence.

By some nights and weekends I am a published researcher — a convolutional neural network for plant-disease classification, 55,000 images, 39 classes, 98.7% validation accuracy, and a paper in IJEECS Vol. 28, No. 3. The habits are different. Research asks you to be wrong slowly and in writing. Engineering asks you to ship by Friday. I find the tension productive.

“The discipline of a researcher and the metabolism of a production engineer, in the same person, is rarer than either alone.” — referee note, paraphrased, 2022

§ 02.3
Fieldwork
2015 — 2019

Before the code, there were the hills. For roughly four years I worked as a guide through Bandarban, Rangamati, Sylhet, and Sunamganj — long days on uncertain trails, reading maps, learning which river crossings were safe that week.

The connection, in retrospect, isn't poetic — it's procedural. You learn to survey unfamiliar terrain, take honest notes, mark what's stable and what isn't, and hand the document to whoever follows. The job has a different name now.²

² The habit of careful documentation was first formed somewhere between a river crossing in Sunamganj and a trail notebook that got rained on. It turns out to be the same habit a senior engineer uses to write a migration plan.
