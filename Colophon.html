§ 09.2  —  Principles that shaped this  
01

A portfolio is a document, not a landing page.

The manual format — paged sections, margin notes, a dateline on every entry — is a commitment. It asks to be read, not skimmed. That constraint rules out hero videos, autoplaying animation, and anything that blinks.

02

Type does the heavy lifting. Not colour or motion.

One serif for reading, one mono for data. Two weights each, one accent colour. Hierarchy comes from size, italics, and rule weight — the same tools a typesetter had a century ago.

03

If I don't know it, I don't display it.

Every metric on every page is either live-fetched or explicitly marked as a placeholder waiting for real data. Fake counters and rounded-off stat-slop would undermine the whole document.

04

Private work gets named, not shown.

The bulk of my hours go to Apple and AccTech under NDA. The manual summarises what I worked on and for whom, without publishing screenshots, internal architecture, or client code. Honest gap over fabricated filler.

05

Readable on a phone in a taxi at midnight.

Everything reflows to a single column, nav rail collapses to a strip, type scales down but never slips under a comfortable reading size. The manual should work as well on a 390-pixel screen as on a 27-inch monitor.
